Every growing engineering organisation hits the same wall. Headcount doubles, but output doesn't. Meetings multiply, decisions slow down, and the engineers who used to ship weekly now spend their days coordinating. We restructured around small, autonomous, cross-functional pods, and velocity recovered while the team kept growing.

What a Pod Is

A pod is a long-lived team of five to eight people that owns an outcome end to end. A typical pod includes:

  • A product lead who owns the problem and the priorities.
  • A product designer embedded full time, not shared across teams.
  • Three to five engineers spanning frontend, backend and infrastructure.
  • A quality engineer who shapes testing strategy rather than running manual tests.

Ownership Beats Coordination

Pods own services, not tickets. They deploy their own code, carry their own pager, and see their own metrics. When the people who build a system also operate it, quality improves without anyone having to mandate it.

“The fastest meeting is the one you don't need because the team already owns the decision.”

Keeping Pods Aligned

Autonomy without alignment becomes chaos. Three lightweight mechanisms keep pods pointing in the same direction:

  1. Quarterly outcomes written as measurable goals, agreed across pods in a single planning session.
  2. Architecture decision records for any change that affects another pod's services.
  3. A weekly written update instead of a status meeting: what shipped, what's blocked, what's next.

Measuring Velocity Honestly

We track the four DORA metrics per pod: deployment frequency, lead time for changes, change failure rate and time to restore service. They're used for improvement conversations, never for comparing pods or individuals.

What Changed

  • Median lead time from merge to production fell from 9 days to under 1 day.
  • Recurring cross-team meetings dropped by more than half.
  • Engineer engagement scores rose for three consecutive quarters.
